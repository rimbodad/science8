3. Core Knowledge — Kiến thức phải nắm chắc

3.1 Habitat destruction

The SB states that human activities destroy natural habitats through activities such as intensive farming and building farms, towns and cities.

Deforestation

Forests cleared→habitats destroyed→species decline / extinction→ecosystem relationships disrupted

The banyan-tree example shows ecosystem interdependence: bats eat banyan fruit and spread seeds; owls and hawks eat bats; other organisms use banyan trees for shelter. If banyan trees disappear, bat numbers can fall and the wider forest ecosystem is affected.

The SB also notes that forests act as carbon sinks, so cutting forests contributes to climate change.

Habitat fragmentation

Habitat fragmentation happens when large areas are divided into smaller fragments by roads, cities or farmland. It can make shelter, food and water harder to find and can lead to loss of biodiversity.

3.2 Overexploitation

ExampleCore idea
OverfishingMore fish are removed than can reproduce, so populations fall.
OverhuntingToo many wild animals are removed from terrestrial habitats.
Possible consequenceSpecies can become endangered or extinct; food supplies/resources for other organisms are reduced.
The SB contrasts traditional fishing, which removes relatively small numbers at a time, with some modern fishing methods that can seriously damage ocean ecosystems.

3.3 Re-introduction

The SB gives the example of wolves re-introduced into Yellowstone National Park. Re-introducing organisms that disappeared from a natural habitat can help rejuvenate an ecosystem.

3.4 Non-native and invasive species

A non-native species is not naturally found in an area. It may upset an ecosystem by bringing disease, using resources that native organisms need, or eating native organisms. A harmful non-native species is called invasive.

Example from SBImpact
Nile perch in Lake VictoriaIntroduced as a food source; as a predator it caused native fish species to become extinct, reducing food for birds and mammals.
Asian hornetsPrey on native bees and reduce bee populations, threatening pollination and biodiversity.
Black and brown ratsHunt native mammals and birds and eat bird eggs; linked to extinction of native species.

3.5 Pollution

Land pollutionGarbage, plastics and chemicals on land; landfills can allow chemicals to seep into soil.
Air pollutionSmoke, car exhaust and construction dust; common pollutants named include sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
Water pollutionChemicals and waste entering ponds, rivers, lakes or oceans from farms, factories, cities and homes.

3.6 Air pollution and acid rain

Sulfur dioxide / nitrogen oxides→dissolve in clouds→acid rain→harms plants & animals

The SB also notes that landfill waste can produce methane, a greenhouse gas that can cause global warming.

3.7 Causes of water pollution

SourceEffect described in SB
Fertilisers & pesticidesCan wash into rivers/lakes; fertilisers may cause algal blooms that block sunlight, killing underwater plants and organisms that depend on them.
Factory chemicalsCan be toxic to plants and animals and enter food/water.
Waste waterMay contain bacteria, oil and pollutants; untreated water can make people/animals sick and harm plants.
Oil spillsOil coats water, feathers and fur; impairs movement, poisons animals and affects shore organisms.
Litter & solid wasteMarine animals can become tangled in plastic or eat it, causing injury or death.
MicroplasticsCan be eaten by marine organisms and enter the food chain.

3.8 Bioaccumulation

Some toxic substances cannot be broken down or excreted. They stay in organisms and build up over time. The SB gives mercury as an example.

Toxic substance enters organism→cannot be broken down / excreted→builds up over time→harm / death possible

3.9 Biomagnification

Plankton: small amount→small fish: larger amount→large fish: even larger amount
Do not confuse:
Bioaccumulation = toxin builds up inside one organism over time.
Biomagnification = toxin level increases along a food chain.

4. Must-Know Relationships — Chuỗi suy luận

Deforestation→habitat loss→population decline→biodiversity loss
Habitat fragmentation→harder to find food/shelter/water→lower survival
Overfishing / overhunting→population falls→endangered / extinct
Invasive species→predation / disease / competition→native populations decline
Fertiliser runoff→algal bloom→sunlight blocked→underwater plants die
Persistent toxin→bioaccumulation→biomagnification along food chain

7. Mastery Test — Tự kiểm tra

Tự trả lời trước khi mở đáp án.

Level 1 — Know

1. What is deforestation?
Xem đáp án
The cutting down and clearing of forests.
2. What is overexploitation?
Xem đáp án
Removing too many animals from the wild, usually for food.
3. What is an invasive species?
Xem đáp án
A non-native species that causes a lot of harm in its new ecosystem.
4. Define bioaccumulation.
Xem đáp án
The slow build-up of toxic substances in a living organism's body.

Level 2 — Understand

5. Why can habitat fragmentation reduce biodiversity?
Xem đáp án
It divides habitats into smaller fragments and makes it harder for animals to find shelter, food or water, reducing survival.
6. Give two ways an invasive species can harm native organisms.
Xem đáp án
It may eat native organisms, spread disease, or use resources that native organisms depend on.
7. Explain how fertiliser runoff can harm an aquatic ecosystem.
Xem đáp án
Extra nutrients can cause an algal bloom. The algae block sunlight, underwater plants die, and animals that depend on them may also die.
8. What is the difference between bioaccumulation and biomagnification?
Xem đáp án
Bioaccumulation is toxin build-up within one organism over time; biomagnification is the increase in toxin amount/concentration along a food chain.

Level 3 — Apply

9. A lake receives mercury pollution. Plankton absorb a little mercury, small fish eat many plankton, and large fish eat many small fish. Which fish are likely to contain the most mercury?
Xem đáp án
The larger fish, because mercury biomagnifies along the food chain.
10. A company accused of polluting a river publishes its own investigation saying it is not responsible. What science issue should you consider?
Xem đáp án
Whether the source is biased, because the company has an interest in the conclusion.
11. A fish population is harvested faster than it can reproduce for many years. Predict the likely long-term result.
Xem đáp án
The population can fall severely and may become endangered or extinct; the fishing industry may collapse.
12. An introduced predator causes a native prey population to fall. What may happen to other predators that also depend on that prey?
Xem đáp án
Their populations may also decrease because there is less food available.

  • I can explain overexploitation, overfishing and overhunting.
  • I can distinguish native, non-native and invasive species.
  • I can explain how invasive species can disrupt food webs and biodiversity.
  • I can describe land, air and water pollution.
  • I can explain acid rain and algal blooms using cause-and-effect chains.
  • I can describe how plastics, microplastics, wastewater and oil spills harm ecosystems.
  • I can define bioaccumulation and biomagnification and distinguish them.
  • I can use graph or food-chain evidence to support or contradict a hypothesis.
